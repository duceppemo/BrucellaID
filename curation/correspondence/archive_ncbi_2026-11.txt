Subject: Third-party curation request: 5 Brucella BioSamples whose organism field is
         contradicted by the deposited reads, plus 10 flagged for annotation

To the NCBI BioSample team,

We have validated a Brucella species-identification tool against every public Brucella
read set in ENA and the SRA - 4,042 runs under taxon 234 - and are reporting the records
where the deposited organism does not match the sequence. We are not the submitter of
any of them. We have written to the submitting groups as well, and will pass on anything
they confirm or dispute.

A separate message about six assembly records went to GenBank on 2026-11-07; this one
concerns BioSamples behind SRA runs and does not overlap with it.

Two requests. Section 1 asks for a change of organism. Section 2 asks only for an
annotation.


1. FIVE BIOSAMPLES: ORGANISM FIELD CONTRADICTED BY THE SEQUENCE

Each rests on two methods sharing no code and no reference data: Mash distance over a
curated 431-genome reference set covering all 30 named species of the clade, and the
nine-locus Whatmore MLST scheme called from the reads. The real-time assays of Hoover
et al. 2026 (doi:10.1128/spectrum.02974-25), run in silico, agree as a third line
wherever the species is one they cover.

  BioSample       runs                          deposited              proposed
  SAMN00811170    SRR642185 SRR651210 SRR867710 B. abortus 63/183      B. suis
  SAMN00811205    SRR867711                     B. melitensis 63/227   B. suis
  SAMN15685105    SRR31560207                   B. abortus             B. melitensis
  SAMN14309819    SRR11526759                   B. abortus             B. melitensis
  SAMN44729562    SRR31357708                   B. cytisi              B. anthropi

  SAMN00811170   Mash 0.0022-0.0023 to B. suis; ST 14 and ST 16, which PubMLST records
                 as B. suis in 96.4% of 140 and all 15 isolates respectively; the
                 B. suis assay is held and the abortus and melitensis assays absent.
                 One BioSample sequenced three times; all three libraries agree.
  SAMN00811205   Mash 0.00205 to B. suis; ST 14; same assay pattern.
  SAMN15685105   Mash 0.00056 to B. melitensis against 0.0016 to any abortus; ST 7,
                 which PubMLST records as B. melitensis in 98.6% of 72 isolates.
  SAMN14309819   Mash 0.00098 to B. melitensis; ST 8, melitensis in 98.3% of 862
                 isolates. Note the BioProject title for PRJNA610713 already reads
                 "Brucella melitensis strain 2308", so only the sample's organism
                 field disagrees. Strain 2308 is conventionally B. abortus 2308, which
                 may be worth raising with the submitter separately; our evidence
                 speaks to the species, not the strain designation.
  SAMN44729562   Distance only, and we flag it as the weakest of the five. The former
                 Ochrobactrum species carry none of the classical MLST alleles and
                 neither PCR panel covers them. Mash 0.0076 to the nearest B. anthropi
                 against 0.0381-0.0394 to the three deposited assemblies of the
                 B. cytisi type strain, which agree with each other to 0.0026. The
                 reliable statement is that the sequence is hard to reconcile with
                 B. cytisi; B. anthropi is the best available reading.

The first two carry strain designations, which is why we would prioritise them: a
designation gets cited and requested, so a wrong species on one propagates further
than on an ordinary isolate.


2. TEN BIOSAMPLES: DEPOSITED AS BRUCELLA, LIBRARY HOLDS SOMETHING ELSE

No organism change is requested. An annotation that the library does not contain the
deposited organism would stop these being downloaded as Brucella.

  runs                                 deposited              what the library holds
  SRR825216 SRR825217 SRR825218        B. pinnipedialis       an uncultured
                                                              Actinomycetaceae
  SRR886241 SRR886242 SRR886243        B. ceti                Streptococcus intermedius
  SRR848042                            B. abortus             Metamycoplasma orale
  SRR25828922                          B. pseudogrignonensis  a Bacillus species

  The three Actinomycetaceae libraries are one organism at 100% ANI to each other,
  1.275 Mb at 33.2% GC, matching a GTDB r220 representative at 99.33% ANI with 365x
  coverage and 81% containment; that representative is itself a metagenome-assembled
  genome, so the organism has no isolate genome anywhere. The S. intermedius
  identification is unambiguous: 100% ANI at 169x with 8092 of 8093 reference k-mers
  present, and 16S identical to the type strain. M. orale is at 98.2% ANI with 16S
  identical to the type strain, in a mixed library. For SRR25828922 we stop at genus:
  95.8% ANI to Bacillus subtilis with 27% containment places it in Bacillus without
  naming the species.


3. TWO RECORDS WHERE THE DEPOSITED SPECIES IS CORRECT - NO ACTION WANTED

Listed only so they are not caught up in the above if this report is actioned in bulk.

  SRR952243 (B. abortus) holds genuine classical Brucella at 12.4x with 94% of a
  reference genome present, under a dominant Ralstonia pickettii. SRR35797377
  (B. melitensis) holds B. melitensis at 7.2x with 97% present, alongside minor
  organisms. Both deposits look right; the libraries are contaminated. A note would
  help a reader, but the organism field should stand. SRR24420454 (B. melitensis) holds
  a third of a Brucella genome at 0.5x and nothing else - too shallow to judge, and we
  request nothing for it.


EVIDENCE AND METHOD

Every per-record verdict, including the records we checked and cleared and the
mislabelled genomes we found in our own reference set in the same pass, is public at
https://github.com/duceppemo/BrucellaID under curation/. Happy to supply assemblies,
allele calls, distance tables or identification output in whatever form is most useful.
If any of this is better directed to pd-help, please let us know and we will resend.

If any reading is disputed we would genuinely like to know. The physical isolate
outranks the sequence, and the sequence outranks our reading of it.
