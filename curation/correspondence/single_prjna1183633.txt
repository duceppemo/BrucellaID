Subject: SRR31357708 (PRJNA1183633) - a Brucella cytisi isolate that types as
         Brucella anthropi

Dear colleagues,

We maintain a species-identification tool for the Brucella clade and have been
validating it against every public read set deposited under that genus in ENA. One
record from your crop root genome resource came up. I appreciate this is a long way
from the subject of your study, so the short version is that one isolate's species
name looks wrong, and nothing about the sequencing does.

    run          BioSample       deposited         the sequence says
    SRR31357708  SAMN44729562    Brucella cytisi   Brucella anthropi

Some context on why this is awkward to call, and what it does and does not establish.
The 2020 taxonomy moved the genus Ochrobactrum into Brucella, so B. cytisi and
B. anthropi are the former Ochrobactrum cytisi and O. anthropi - plant- and
soil-associated organisms rather than the classical pathogens the genus is named for.
That half of the clade carries none of the classical Brucella MLST alleles, and
neither the Bruce-ladder nor the real-time assays we use cover it, so distance is the
only method available here.

What the distances show, over a curated 431-genome reference set covering all 30 named
species of the clade:

  - 0.0076 from the nearest B. anthropi genome, and all eight nearest matches are
    B. anthropi.
  - 0.0381, 0.0383 and 0.0394 from the three deposited assemblies of the B. cytisi
    type strain (ESC1, LMG 22713, DSM 19778). Those three agree with each other to
    0.0026, so they form a tight cluster your isolate is not in; they rank 89th, 90th
    and 91st of the 431 references.
  - The library assembles to 4.84 Mb, which is the size range of the former
    Ochrobactrum species, so the isolate is at least in the right part of the clade.

The reliable conclusion is the negative one: the sequence is hard to reconcile with
B. cytisi. B. anthropi is the best available reading, but within this group the
within-species spread is very wide - inside deposited B. anthropi it reaches 0.21 -
so I would not argue hard for anthropi over another neighbour on 0.0076 alone. If
your pipeline assigned the name automatically, that may be the simplest explanation;
these two species are close and the reference data for them is uneven, which is
something we have been documenting.

Nothing here affects the rest of your resource, and I am not suggesting any of it
needs revisiting. If you would like the record corrected, BioSample organism changes
are handled by NCBI at biosamplehelp@ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, quoting SAMN44729562; we are
glad to send the evidence to them directly instead if that is easier.

If you disagree with the reading I would genuinely like to know. The isolate outranks
the sequence, and the sequence outranks our reading of it.

Every per-record verdict is public at https://github.com/duceppemo/BrucellaID under
curation/, including the mislabelled genomes we found in our own reference set in the
same pass.
