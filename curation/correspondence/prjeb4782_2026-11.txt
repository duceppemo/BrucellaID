Subject: PRJEB4782 - three BioSamples whose deposited species differs from the
         sequence, and ten runs that appear not to hold Brucella

Dear colleagues,

We have been validating a Brucella species-identification tool against every public
Brucella read set in ENA, and PRJEB4782 came up. One hundred and forty-three of your
156 runs came back exactly as deposited. Thirteen did not, in two quite different
ways, and the second group is the reason I am writing rather than just filing a
metadata note.

METHOD, BRIEFLY

Mash distance over a curated 431-genome reference set covering all 30 named species of
the clade, and MLST-9 (Whatmore et al.) called from the reads and read off the
assemblies by exact allele match. The two share no code and no reference data. The
real-time assays of Hoover et al. 2026 (doi:10.1128/spectrum.02974-25), run in silico,
agree where they cover the species in question.

1. THREE BIOSAMPLES WITH A DIFFERENT SPECIES

    run          BioSample       deposited      sequence says    Mash     ST
    ERR471314    SAMEA2266858    B. ceti        B. abortus       0.00017  5
    ERR471315    SAMEA2266859    B. ceti        B. abortus       0.00017  5
    ERR473742    SAMEA2266954    B. abortus     B. neotomae      0.00034  22

PubMLST records ST5 as B. abortus (98.6% of 71 isolates) and ST22 as B. neotomae
(100% of 9). In silico Bruce-ladder independently gives the same species for all
three, and the species-specific real-time assay fires correctly for the two
B. abortus readings. All three are tight matches, not borderline calls.

The two B. ceti records are consecutive accessions and consecutive BioSamples, so they
may be one mix-up rather than two.

2. TEN RUNS THAT DO NOT APPEAR TO HOLD BRUCELLA

These are deposited as B. abortus and share one or two of 1000 k-mer hashes with any
genome in the Brucella clade. A real Brucella library at usable depth shares 900 or
more. The genus marker of the real-time panel is negative on both the reads and their
assemblies, and in silico Bruce-ladder finds nothing.

We also checked these by containment rather than by abundance, which is the fairer
test for a minority organism: profiling the reads against GTDB r220 finds Brucella in
nine of the ten at 0.03-0.17x coverage with only 4-13% of the reference genome
present, which is the level of index hopping or trace carry-over rather than a real
population. The tenth, ERR775246, reaches 0.43x and 31%, still far short of a genome
but worth mentioning for completeness.

We have identified what they are, and the ten fall into three groups, each internally
consistent:

    ERR471337    4,449,368    Camelimonas fluminis             98.35% ANI to the type
    ERR554820    4,503,334    Camelimonas fluminis             98.35% ANI to the type
    ERR775236    4,423,614    Camelimonas fluminis             98.39% ANI to the type
    ERR473743    3,953,140    Camelimonas, unnamed species     95.5% ANI to Camelimonas
    ERR775237    3,954,007    Camelimonas, unnamed species       sp. ID_303_24; 89.6%
                                                                 to the fluminis type
    ERR775244    1,913,108    Alcaligenaceae, unnamed genus    16S 94% to the Advenella
    ERR775245    1,884,007    Alcaligenaceae, unnamed genus      kashmirensis type; no
    ERR775246    1,876,705    Alcaligenaceae, unnamed genus      hit in any genome
    ERR775247    1,883,342    Alcaligenaceae, unnamed genus      database we tried
    ERR775248    1,912,218    Alcaligenaceae, unnamed genus

Methods: mashID against proGenomes v4 (32,887 representative genomes) and against a
dereplicated RefSeq bacterial sketch; fastANI against all ten Camelimonas genomes in
NCBI; and 16S rRNA extracted with barrnap and compared with the NCBI 16S type-strain
database and with nt. The four methods agree.

The first five are probably the most useful part for you. Camelimonas is in
Brucellaceae, the same family as Brucella - a slow-growing, oxidase-positive
alphaproteobacterium, and the genus even contains a Camelimonas abortus. An isolate
from a brucellosis workup identified by biochemical or serological methods could
very reasonably have been recorded as Brucella. The reference our two unnamed-species
libraries match best, Camelimonas sp. ID_303_24, is itself a clinical isolate from
human blood, and its nearest relative at NCBI came from a vertebral biopsy, so this
appears to be a group that turns up in human clinical material.

The last five are one organism: they are 99.3-100% ANI to each other, all 52.7% GC,
all 1.88-1.91 Mb, and assembled at 250-270x, so the genome really is that small
rather than half-covered. Its full-length 16S is only 94% identical to its nearest
named relatives, which is below genus-level similarity, and it has no match at all in
either genome database - not a weak match, none. On present evidence it is a member
of the family Alcaligenaceae with no sequenced representative, which may make it
interesting in its own right if you still hold the isolate.

I can send the assemblies, the 16S sequences or the full comparison tables.

These ten are the reason for the email rather than a metadata correction: someone
searching the archive for B. abortus reads will find and download them, which is how
we did.

WHAT WOULD HELP

For the three in section 1, updating the organism field on the BioSamples would
correct them downstream. For the ten in section 2, a check of what was uploaded
against what was intended would be worth more than any change to the labels - if the
wrong files went up, the right ones may still exist.

If you disagree with any of the readings I would genuinely like to know. The physical
isolate outranks the sequence, and the sequence outranks our reading of it.

Every per-record verdict is public at https://github.com/duceppemo/BrucellaID under
curation/, including the records we checked and cleared and the errors we found in our
own reference set in the same pass. Happy to send assemblies, allele calls or distance
tables directly.

With thanks for depositing the data in the first place - 143 of 156 is a better record
than most, and none of this would be checkable otherwise.
