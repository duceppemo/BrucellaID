Subject: Eleven BioSamples in PRJEB30030 whose deposited species differs from the
         sequence

Dear colleagues,

We have been validating a Brucella species-identification tool against every public
Brucella read set in ENA, and PRJEB30030 came up. Eighty-seven of your 98 runs came
back exactly as deposited. Eleven did not, and I am writing because the BioSample
organism field is yours to amend rather than the archive's, and because a couple of
the patterns may be more useful to you than to us.

Nothing here is a comment on the sequencing, which is good: ten of the eleven
libraries assemble to 3.24-3.35 Mb, the normal size for a Brucella genome. These look
like sound genomes with a different species name on them.

METHOD, BRIEFLY

Two independent lines, which is why I am raising it rather than filing it. Mash
distance over a curated 432-genome reference set covering all 30 named species of the
clade, and MLST-9 (Whatmore et al.) called from the reads and read off the assemblies
by exact allele match. The two share no code and no reference data. Where the
real-time assays of Hoover et al. 2026 (doi:10.1128/spectrum.02974-25) cover the
species in question, run in silico, they agree as well.

PubMLST records these sequence types as: ST1 B. abortus (99.4% of 466 isolates), ST5
B. abortus (98.6% of 71), ST14 B. suis (96.4% of 140), ST15 B. suis (96.1% of 103),
ST19 B. suis (100% of 6), ST22 B. neotomae (100% of 9), ST25 B. pinnipedialis (95.0%
of 80). ERR2993145 has no ST called, but both of our reference sets reach B. suis for
it independently.

Six of the ten sit 0.0001-0.0003 from the species the sequence indicates, which is as
close as two genomes of one species ever get, so these are not borderline.

ONE PATTERN YOU MAY BE ABLE TO CHECK

Ten of the eleven are deposited as B. melitensis, but the sequences fall into four
different species. If the labels had drifted one isolate at a time we would expect
the errors to be scattered in both directions; a single deposited name against four
true species looks more like a name applied across a batch than per isolate. The two
accession blocks also behave differently: the ERR29931xx group sits at
0.00007-0.00026 while the ERR29386xx group sits at 0.00081-0.00244. You will know far
better than we would whether those correspond to separate runs, plates or submission
batches.

ONE RECORD I WOULD NOT CALL A MISLABEL

    ERR2938687   SAMEA5145007    B. melitensis     probably a mixture

This one has the largest Mash distance of the eleven (0.00591), by far the worst
assembly (2,165 contigs against 26-80 for the others), and in the reads it carries a
complete B. suis amplicon in 54% of informative reads and a complete B. melitensis
amplicon in 33%. A mixed culture or a contaminated library explains all three
observations together; a wrong species name explains none of them well. I would
suggest re-examining the isolate rather than changing the record.

WHAT WOULD HELP

If you agree with the readings, updating the organism field on the ten BioSamples
would correct them downstream - they are currently feeding reference databases, which
is how we met them.

ENA has no third-party route for metadata corrections, so if you would rather we
raised it than you did, it goes through the support form at
https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ena/browser/support or the ELIXIR Clearinghouse, which exists
for third-party curation of records one did not submit. We are glad to do that with
the evidence attached.

If you disagree on any, I would genuinely like to know: the physical isolate outranks
the sequence, and the sequence outranks our reading of it.

Every per-record verdict is public at https://github.com/duceppemo/BrucellaID under
curation/, including the records we checked and cleared and the errors we found in our
own reference set in the same pass. Happy to send the assemblies, the allele calls or
the distance tables directly.

With thanks for depositing the data in the first place - none of this would be
checkable otherwise.
