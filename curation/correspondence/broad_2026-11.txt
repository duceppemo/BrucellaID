Subject: Ten Brucella sequencing records from 2012-2013 - nine whose deposited
         species does not match the sequence, and one contaminated library

Dear colleagues,

We have been validating a Brucella species-identification tool against every public
Brucella read set in ENA. Ten records deposited by the Broad Institute between
December 2012 and August 2013, across six studies, are worth a look - two BioSamples
and eight runs, counting SAMN00811170 once rather than once per library. Nine do not
match their deposited species; the tenth, SRR952243, does match, but the library is
dominated by a contaminant and I have set it apart below. Of the 19 runs in those
studies we were able to analyse 14; the other five fell outside our size filters.

I am conscious these are thirteen-year-old records from an early-generation project
and that the material behind them may be long gone. I am not expecting anyone to
re-sequence anything. The reason for writing is that the records are still being
consumed - they are feeding reference databases, which is how we met them - so a
metadata correction or even a note on the record would be worth more than its effort.

Two different problems, and they need different handling.

METHOD, BRIEFLY

Mash distance over a curated 432-genome reference set covering all 30 named species of
the Brucella clade; MLST-9 (Whatmore et al.) called from reads and read off assemblies
by exact allele match; in silico Bruce-ladder, Suis-ladder and AMOS PCR; and the
real-time assays of Hoover et al. 2026 (doi:10.1128/spectrum.02974-25) run in silico.
For the libraries that are not Brucella at all we added mashID against proGenomes v4
and a dereplicated RefSeq sketch, sylph against GTDB r220, fastANI, and 16S rRNA
extracted with barrnap and compared with the NCBI type-strain database and nt.


1. TWO NAMED REFERENCE STRAINS THAT TYPE AS B. SUIS

These two matter more than their number suggests, because both carry strain
designations. A strain designation gets cited, requested and reused, so a wrong
species on one travels further than a wrong species on an ordinary isolate.

    BioSample      runs                            deposited             Mash      ST
    SAMN00811170   SRR642185 SRR651210 SRR867710   B. abortus 63/183    0.0022   14,16
    SAMN00811205   SRR867711                       B. melitensis 63/227  0.00205   14

    Both read as B. suis. PubMLST records ST14 as B. suis in 96.4% of 140 isolates and
    ST16 as B. suis in all 15 it has. The B. suis real-time assay is held in full and
    the B. abortus and B. melitensis assays are absent; in silico Suis-ladder and
    Bruce-ladder give suis patterns.

    SAMN00811170 is one BioSample sequenced three times rather than three records. All
    three libraries agree, which is a consistency check rather than three independent
    findings - and a useful one, because two of the three assemble well and the third
    badly, and all three still read as B. suis.

    If strains 63/183 and 63/227 are held in a collection, the deposited material may
    be worth checking too: the sequence suggests the mix-up predates the sequencing.


2. SEVEN LIBRARIES THAT DO NOT CONTAIN THE DEPOSITED ORGANISM, AND ONE THAT DOES

Seven of these eight share nothing meaningful with any Brucella genome - between zero
and 146 of 1000 k-mer hashes, where a real Brucella library at usable depth shares 900
or more. We identified what they do contain, since "not Brucella" is less useful than
a name. The eighth, SRR952243, is the exception and is not a mislabel at all: its
deposited species looks right and the library is simply dominated by something else.
It is last below, and I would treat it separately.

  PRJNA52097 - SRR825216, SRR825217, SRR825218, deposited as B. pinnipedialis

    An uncultured member of the family Actinomycetaceae. The three libraries are the
    same organism (100% ANI to each other), 1.275 Mb at 33.2% GC, 995 predicted genes
    at 89% coding density, so a real reduced genome rather than a fragment.

    It matches a GTDB r220 representative at 99.33% ANI with 365x coverage and 81% of
    that genome's k-mers present. That representative, GCA_946998115.1, is itself a
    metagenome-assembled genome from a human microbiome study - 1.08 Mb at 32.5% GC -
    so this organism has no isolate genome anywhere, which is why it is absent from
    proGenomes v4 and from a dereplicated RefSeq sketch while being present in GTDB.
    Its 16S is 100% identical to a reference deposited as Pauljensenia (formerly
    Actinomyces) hongkongensis, though that species' own genome is 70.5% GC, so we
    would not put a species name on it.

  PRJNA197004 - SRR886241, SRR886242, SRR886243, deposited as B. ceti

    Streptococcus intermedius, and this one is not ambiguous: 100% ANI at 169x
    coverage with 8092 of 8093 reference k-mers present, 16S 100% identical, and
    assemblies of 1.91 Mb at 37.6% GC matching the species. Oral flora.

  PRJNA77813 - SRR848042, deposited as B. abortus

    Metamycoplasma orale (formerly Mycoplasma orale), at 98.2% ANI and 44x coverage,
    with the 16S 100% identical to M. orale NCTC 10112. The library is a mixture: the
    assembly runs to 17.7 Mb in 18,776 contigs. M. orale is among the commonest
    cell-culture contaminants.

  PRJNA213741 - SRR952243, deposited as B. abortus

    This one is NOT a mislabel, and I would treat it separately. The library is
    dominated by Ralstonia pickettii - 98.0% ANI, 129x, 16S 100% identical, and a
    clean 5.4 Mb assembly at 63.7% GC - but it also contains genuine classical
    Brucella at 12.4x coverage with 94% of the reference genome present. Our own
    read-level PCR independently finds the genus marker and the B. abortus assay in
    the reads while finding nothing in the assembly, because the Brucella is
    outnumbered.

    So the deposited species looks right and the library is contaminated. R. pickettii
    is the classic ultrapure-water and reagent organism. A note on the record would
    serve a reader better than a relabel.


WHAT WOULD HELP

For section 1, correcting the organism field on the two BioSamples, since that is the
submitter's to amend rather than the archive's. For section 2, a note on each record
that the library does not contain the deposited organism would stop the next person
downloading them as Brucella; if the original files still exist somewhere, a check of
what was uploaded against what was intended may be worth more.

To correct the record: BioSample organism changes are handled by NCBI's help desk at
biosamplehelp@ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, quoting the accession, the current name and the
proposed one. We are glad to send the evidence to them directly if that is easier.

If you disagree with any of these readings I would genuinely like to know. The
physical isolate outranks the sequence, and the sequence outranks our reading of it.

Every per-record verdict is public at https://github.com/duceppemo/BrucellaID under
curation/, including the records we checked and cleared and the errors we found in our
own reference set in the same pass. Happy to send assemblies, allele calls, distance
tables or the 16S sequences directly.
